    Hosting Piler for your customers#

    This documentation applies to Piler enterprise edition 2.2.5+

    Revision #2

    Publication date: 2026-OCT-27

    How a hosting provider or MSP runs Piler for many customers: one multi-tenant deployment, a tenant per customer, each with its own hostname, users and archive.

    Who does what#

    Piler The hosting partner
    The software: piler, piler-ui, piler-smtp and the tools, updates and security fixes Hosting: the servers, storage, network, backups, monitoring
    Documentation, the example deployments First-line support to the customers
    Email support to the partner, business hours, best effort SLAs, migrations from other archives, onboarding projects

    Piler sells software, not a service: there is no SLA from Piler itself. You set your own prices and terms for your customers.

    Licensing: a multi-tenant license is priced per server, regardless of the number of tenants and users. Each worker node and the frontend node has its own license. The more tenants a server holds, the lower your cost per tenant, see sizing and the multi-tenant pricing.

    Several master nodes#

    Several master nodes (piler-ui) behind a load balancer need leader election. Set on every master:

    LEADER_ELECTION_ENABLED=true

    They must use the same Redis (they need it for the sessions anyway) and the same LEADER_REDIS_KEY (default piler:leader). One master, the leader, runs the background jobs: the billing report, the quota numbers collected every 15 minutes (from 2.2.5), the quota warning emails, the Teams retention purge. If it stops, another master takes over within about 35 seconds (LEADER_LOCK_TTL 30s, LEADER_RETRY_INTERVAL 5s).

    Without it, which is the default, every master runs every job: your tenants may get the quota warning email from each master, each master sends the billing report and asks every worker for the quota of every tenant, and the Teams purges run at the same time.

    With a single master, leave it off.
