    GoBD Compliance for Email Archiving#

    This documentation applies to Piler enterprise edition 2.1.0+

    Revision #4

    Publication date: 2026-OCT-30

    Deutsche Fassung

    Legal notice

    This documentation describes how piler enterprise supports compliance with the German GoBD requirements from a technical and organizational perspective.

    It does not constitute legal advice and does not replace the organization's obligation to assess its own legal requirements, risks, and compliance measures.

    Organizations remain solely responsible for:

    • determining which emails are tax-relevant or commercially relevant,
    • configuring appropriate retention periods,
    • ensuring proper access controls and audit trails,
    • maintaining overall compliance with German tax and commercial law.

    piler enterprise provides technical means to support, but not substitute, regulatory and legal decision-making.

    The references "Rz." are the margin numbers of the GoBD, in the version of 14 July 2025 (see References).

    What is GoBD?#

    GoBD (Grundsätze zur ordnungsmäßigen Führung und Aufbewahrung von Büchern, Aufzeichnungen und Unterlagen in elektronischer Form sowie zum Datenzugriff) is an administrative regulation of the German Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF). The current version is the BMF letter of 28 November 2019, amended on 11 March 2024 and on 14 July 2025 (mainly for mandatory e-invoicing).

    It defines requirements for:

    • Electronic bookkeeping and record-keeping
    • Retention of tax-relevant documents in electronic form
    • Data access rights for tax authorities during audits

    GoBD applies to all businesses subject to German tax law, regardless of size. Non-compliance can result in:

    • Rejection of bookkeeping by tax authorities
    • Estimated tax assessments
    • Fines and penalties
    • Increased audit scrutiny

    Two principles of the GoBD shape what an archive can and can't do for you:

    • The responsibility stays with the company, also when the archive is run by a service provider or a hosting partner (Rz. 21).
    • There is no GoBD certificate. The tax authority makes no general statements about the conformity of a software product, and certificates or attestations of third parties have no binding effect on it (Rz. 179 to 181). What counts is how the archive is configured and operated, and the procedure documentation that describes it (Rz. 151 to 155).

    Legal basis#

    The GoBD requirements are derived from several German laws:

    Law Requirement
    § 147 AO (Abgabenordnung) Retention of tax-relevant documents
    § 257 HGB (Handelsgesetzbuch) Retention of commercial correspondence
    § 14b UStG Retention of invoices for VAT purposes
    GoBD (BMF letter) Technical implementation requirements

    Retention periods#

    Document type Retention period
    Books and records, inventories, annual financial statements, management reports, and the work instructions and organizational documents needed to understand them 10 years
    Accounting vouchers (Buchungsbelege), including invoices 8 years (10 years until 2024)
    Commercial and business letters received and sent (offers, order confirmations, general business correspondence) 6 years

    The retention period begins at the end of the calendar year in which the document was created or received.

    The period for accounting vouchers was shortened from 10 to 8 years by the Fourth Bureaucracy Relief Act (BEG IV) from 1 January 2025 (§ 147 Abs. 3 AO, § 257 Abs. 4 HGB, § 14b UStG). Banks, insurance companies and investment firms keep 10 years for a transition period. Many tax advisers still recommend keeping vouchers for 10 years. The retention period of an email is also not always clear from its subject: ask your tax adviser which periods to configure.

    Which emails must be archived?#

    What matters is the content and the function of an email, not what it's called (Rz. 121). An email that serves as a business letter or an accounting voucher must be kept. An email that is only the "transport" of an attached invoice, and contains nothing else that must be kept, doesn't have to be (like a paper envelope).

    Tax-relevant emails#

    • Invoices (incoming and outgoing)
    • Invoice corrections and credit notes
    • Contracts with financial implications
    • Payment confirmations and reminders
    • Tax declarations and correspondence with tax authorities
    • Financial statements and accounting documents

    Commercially relevant emails#

    • Offers and quotations
    • Order confirmations
    • Delivery notes and shipping confirmations
    • General business correspondence
    • Customer and supplier communications with commercial significance

    Emails that do NOT require archiving#

    • Private correspondence (if permitted by company policy)
    • Spam and advertising
    • Newsletters without business relevance
    • Internal informal communications without commercial significance
    • Drafts that were never sent (Rz. 5)

    Separating these reliably by hand is rarely practical. Most companies archive every email of their domains with a journal rule and set the retention period by the longest applicable obligation, with retention rules for exceptions.

    Technical implementation#

    Completeness#

    GoBD requires that business transactions are recorded completely and without gaps (Rz. 36). For an email archive, this means that every email that must be kept reaches the archive.

    How Piler ensures completeness:

    • A journal rule on the mail server sends a copy of every incoming and outgoing email to Piler, without relying on users or clients
    • Real-time archiving: emails are archived as they arrive
    • Headers and attachments are kept with the email, and the envelope recipients (e.g. Bcc recipients) with envelope files
    • Nothing in the email is modified during archiving

    Configure your mail server to send a journal copy of all emails to the Piler SMTP daemon, see the documentation of your mail server or Microsoft 365 journaling.

    Immutability and logging#

    A record must not be changed in a way that its original content can no longer be determined, and changes must be logged (§ 146 Abs. 4 AO, Rz. 58 to 60 and 107 to 112). Immutability can be ensured by hardware (e.g. WORM storage), by software (e.g. locks, logging, versioning) or by organizational measures (e.g. access rights). Storing files in a plain file system is regularly not enough without additional measures (Rz. 110).

    How Piler ensures immutability:

    • Emails are stored in their original format (EML), as received, immediately upon receipt
    • Piler has no function to edit an archived email: users and administrators can only read, export or (if enabled) request its removal
    • All stored emails are encrypted at rest with AES
    • A SHA-256 digest of each email is stored when it's archived, and verified when the email is opened: a changed email is shown as not verified
    • Audit logs record access and administrative actions, see below
    • Optional: TSA timestamps. The sign tool has the digests of the archived emails signed by a time stamping authority (RFC 3161, sign --tsa-url https://...), as cryptographic proof that they existed unchanged at that time. Run it regularly, e.g. daily from cron
    • Optional: WORM storage, e.g. S3 storage with Object Lock, for immutability at the storage level

    What the audit log records (Rz. 111, 117):

    • Authentication: logins (successful and failed), logouts, passkeys
    • Access: which user viewed, downloaded or restored which email, when, from which IP address
    • Searches: what was searched, and by whom
    • Exports: eDiscovery exports, downloads
    • Removals: removal requests, their approval or rejection, GDPR erasure requests
    • Configuration (2.2.5+): every change made through the web UI or the API, with the new values (never a password): users, groups, domains, LDAP, profiles, tenants, quotas, retention rules, categories, legal holds, DLP and compliance rules, import jobs, branding, API tokens, Teams and Slack retention

    Changes outside Piler (e.g. the .env settings or the mail server's journal rule) aren't in the audit log: record them in the change history of your procedure documentation (Rz. 154). Before 2.2.5, changes of domains, groups, LDAP, profiles and some other settings weren't logged.

    Audit log protection:

    • Audit logs are stored separately from email content, and can't be changed or deleted in the web UI or the API
    • Logs can be forwarded to an external SIEM (Splunk, Sumo Logic, Syslog), see SIEM integration: a copy outside Piler that local administrators can't change

    Retention in the received format#

    Incoming electronic business letters and accounting vouchers must be kept in the format in which they were received (Rz. 119, 131). Converting an email to PDF loses its header, and with it the proof of how the email was received (Rz. 129).

    How Piler meets it:

    • Piler keeps the original email (EML) with all headers and attachments, as received
    • Attachments are never converted: a PDF stays a PDF, an XML invoice stays XML, and a ZUGFeRD invoice keeps its embedded XML
    • The search index and the database are derived from the stored email, which remains the original

    Retention and deletion#

    Documents must not be deleted before the end of their retention period (Rz. 119).

    How Piler supports it:

    • Deletion is disabled by default (ENABLE_DELETE=false): nobody can remove an archived email before its retention period ends
    • Retention period per tenant (the default for all emails), and retention rules for exceptions, by domain, sender, recipient, subject, body, size, attachment name or type
    • Legal hold: emails of the custodians under a legal hold are not purged, even after their retention period
    • Purge: removes emails after their retention period has ended (if enabled for the tenant)

    If you enable deletion (ENABLE_DELETE=true), e.g. for GDPR erasure requests: auditors (and, with ENABLE_DELETE_FOR_USERS=true, users) can request the removal of an email. With NEED_TO_APPROVE_DELETE=true, a data officer must approve each request (four-eyes principle). Every request, approval and rejection is in the audit log. Note that the retention obligations of tax and commercial law generally take precedence over a GDPR erasure request (Art. 17 Abs. 3 lit. b GDPR).

    Recommended configuration for GoBD:

    • Keep ENABLE_DELETE=false, or enable it only with NEED_TO_APPROVE_DELETE=true
    • Set the retention period of the tenant by the longest obligation that applies to your emails (e.g. 10 years, 3650 days)
    • Add a year: Piler counts the period from the date of the email, while the legal period ends at the end of the calendar year (e.g. 11 years, 4018 days, for 10 years)
    • Use retention rules only for emails you know to be shorter-lived (e.g. newsletters)

    Order and index#

    Documents must be kept in order, and every electronic document needs a unique index that stays linked to it for the whole retention period (Rz. 117, 122).

    How Piler supports it:

    • Every email has a unique id, and the Message-ID and the digest are stored with it
    • Full-text search across email bodies, subjects and attachments
    • Metadata search by sender, recipient, date range, size, attachment type
    • Categories, tags and notes for classification; tags and notes are stored apart from the email, which stays unchanged (Rz. 123)
    • Saved searches for recurring queries
    • The search index can be rebuilt from the stored emails (reindex)

    Machine evaluation#

    Archived documents must be machine-evaluable to the extent of their information (Rz. 125 to 128). Full-text search counts as machine evaluation (Rz. 126). Structured data, such as the XML of an e-invoice, must stay available as such (Rz. 127, 129).

    Piler export capabilities:

    Format Use case
    EML The original email; standard and portable
    MBOX Multiple emails in one file
    PDF A readable copy of an email
    CSV Metadata for spreadsheet analysis
    eDiscovery exports Emails with load files and hashes for review tools, see eDiscovery

    Encryption and keys#

    Encrypted documents must be available decrypted, and the keys must be kept as long as documents encrypted with them must be kept (Rz. 134).

    • Piler shows and exports emails decrypted to authorized users
    • Keep the encryption key (piler.key) for the whole retention period, in your backups and in a safe place apart from them. Without it, the archived emails can't be read

    Data security#

    The archive must be protected against loss and unauthorized changes, and the backup procedure is part of the procedure documentation (Rz. 103 to 106).

    • Access control with roles, LDAP / Active Directory, SAML or local accounts, and two-factor authentication
    • Backups are your task: follow the 3-2-1 rule and test restores regularly, see backup and disaster recovery

    E-invoices#

    Since 1 January 2025, businesses in Germany must be able to receive e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions, and issuing them becomes mandatory in stages until 2028. The GoBD amendment of 14 July 2025 sets how they are kept:

    Requirement Rz. Piler
    Incoming e-invoices are kept in the format received, e.g. XML 131 The email is kept as received, the XML attachment unchanged
    For an e-invoice, keeping the structured part (the XML) is enough; for structured data, matching content is required, not a matching image 118, 119, 131 The XML is kept, and the PDF as well
    The human-readable part (e.g. the PDF of a ZUGFeRD invoice) must also be kept if it has additional tax-relevant information (e.g. booking notes or qualified electronic signatures) 119, 125, 131 The whole email is kept, PDF included
    The XML of a hybrid invoice must not be lost by a conversion (e.g. PDF/A-3 to TIFF) 125, 129 Attachments are never converted
    E-invoices are structured data that can be evaluated mathematically and technically 127 The XML is available unchanged for evaluation and export
    An email that only transports an e-invoice doesn't have to be kept 121 Piler keeps it anyway, with the invoice

    Piler keeps e-invoices as the GoBD require because it archives every email as received: XML, PDF and hybrid formats (ZUGFeRD, Factur-X, XRechnung) are all preserved unchanged, and found by full-text search and by attachment type.

    Finding e-invoices (2.2.5+): Piler recognizes the emails with an e-invoice attachment when it archives them: XRechnung and other UBL or CII invoices (XML), and ZUGFeRD / Factur-X invoices (PDF with an embedded XML invoice). Search for them with attachment:einvoice, or the "E-invoice" checkbox of the advanced search. For a tax audit, combine it with a date range and export the results (Z2 / Z3, see Data access in a tax audit). The emails archived before 2.2.5 are found after a reindex.

    Procedural documentation (Verfahrensdokumentation)#

    For every IT system there must be a clearly structured procedure documentation, from which the content, structure, process and results of the procedure are fully and conclusively apparent, and which a knowledgeable third party can review in a reasonable time (Rz. 151). For electronic documents it describes the process from their creation through indexing, processing and storage, finding them, machine evaluation, protection against loss and falsification, to their reproduction (Rz. 152).

    Required elements#

    It usually consists of (Rz. 153):

    1. General description

      • The purpose of the archive, which emails are archived
      • Responsibilities: who operates, administers and audits the archive

    2. User documentation

      • How users and auditors search, view and export emails
      • Roles and access rights

    3. Technical system documentation

      • Software used (Piler enterprise, its version), hardware or hosting, storage locations
      • How emails are captured (journal rule), stored, encrypted and verified
      • Retention periods and rules, deletion settings

    4. Operations documentation

      • Backups and restores, the safekeeping of the encryption key
      • Updates and their history, monitoring
      • The internal control system: access reviews, audit log reviews

    The documentation must match the system actually in use, including the versions of the programs, and changes must be versioned with a traceable history (Rz. 154). It must be kept as long as the documents it explains (Rz. 154). A missing or insufficient procedure documentation is not a formal defect leading to the rejection of the bookkeeping, as long as it doesn't impair traceability and verifiability (Rz. 155).

    Piler supports your procedural documentation with:

    • A template for the procedure documentation (in German), with the parts about Piler filled in

    • This page and the documentation of your Piler version, for the technical parts

    • The audit log, for the controls (logins, access, removals, rule changes)
    • The retention rules and their change history

    Deployment recommendations for GoBD compliance#

    Minimum requirements#

    1. Archive every email

      • Configure a journal rule on the mail server for all incoming and outgoing email

    2. Configure retention

      • The retention period of the tenant by the longest obligation that applies (e.g. 10 years)
      • Retention rules only for emails you know to be shorter-lived

    3. Keep deletion disabled

      • ENABLE_DELETE=false (the default), or deletion only with NEED_TO_APPROVE_DELETE=true

    4. Protect the key and back up

      • Back up the databases, the stored emails and the encryption key; follow the 3-2-1 rule
      • Test restores regularly

    5. Use and review the audit log

      • Review it regularly, as part of your internal control system

    6. Write the procedure documentation

      • See above

    Enhanced compliance#

    1. TSA timestamps: run the sign tool regularly, for independent proof of when the emails were archived
    2. WORM storage: S3 Object Lock or equivalent, for immutability at the storage level
    3. External audit log storage: SIEM integration, so local administrators can't change the logs
    4. Regular reviews: check the configuration against this page and update the procedure documentation after every change

    Data access in a tax audit#

    In a tax audit, the tax authority may access the data in three ways, and choose which (Rz. 163, 164):

    Access Rz. What it means With Piler
    Z1: direct access 165 The auditor reads the data in your system, with read-only access, on site; no online remote access An auditor account for the tax auditor
    Z2: indirect access 166 You (or a service provider) evaluate the data by the auditor's criteria, and hand over the results or give read-only access to them Searches by the auditor's criteria, exported for them
    Z3: data handover 167 to 169 You hand over the data in a machine-readable format, e.g. on a data carrier Export of the selected emails in EML (with their metadata)

    The scope: data access covers the documents that must be kept, within the scope of the audit (Rz. 158, 159). Deciding which data are tax-relevant is your task (Rz. 161).

    An auditor account sees every archived email of the tenant. Piler has no auditor role limited to the tax-relevant emails. If the tax auditor shouldn't see everything, prefer Z2 or Z3: search for the relevant emails yourself and export them. Agree on the format with the auditor.

    An auditor account for direct access (Z1)#

    1. Create a user account with the auditor role, only for the time of the audit
    2. Keep deletion disabled: with ENABLE_DELETE=true, auditors can request removals
    3. Give the credentials to the tax auditor
    4. Follow the access in the audit log
    5. Disable the account after the audit

    All auditor access is logged.
